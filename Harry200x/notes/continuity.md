# Continuity ledger

Update after every chapter. Facts fixed in prose are binding on later chapters.

## Dates
- Harry James Potter born 31 July 1980. Dudley Dursley born 23 June 1980.
- Scar night: 31 October 1981. Harry left on the step at 4 Privet Drive the next night; Petunia finds him the following morning.

## People
- Vernon Dursley: director at Grunnings (drills). Petunia Dursley. Dudley. Marge Dursley visits; bulldogs.
- Arabella Figg: Squib; Wisteria Walk, two streets from Privet Drive; cats Mr Tibbles (big, flat-faced, yellow eyes; stares), Snowy, Mr Paws (not well), Tufty (shy, under the sideboard); house smells of cabbage and cats, gas fire, photographs of cats on every surface including the television; string bag; ordinary voice dry and a little cracked. Reports to Dumbledore. Talks to Harry the way she talks to the cats: a good deal, dry, without waiting for an answer. No baby voice.
- Yvonne: Petunia's friend, minded Dudley the night of the Grunnings dinner (Dec 1982).
- Albus Dumbledore: great ally from chapter 4. Condition: at least three weeks a year at Privet Drive.
- Minerva McGonagall: Harry saw her turn into a cat on the night he arrived (every stage visible to him).
- Hagrid: carried Harry from Godric's Hollow; wept.
- Sirius Black: held Harry at Godric's Hollow; Harry keeps his face exactly. Not yet named to Harry.
- Remus Lupin: enters chapter 6.

## The house
- 4 Privet Drive: front room with television, kitchen, hall with cupboard under the stairs, four bedrooms (Vernon and Petunia; Dudley; Dudley's second room full of junk where Harry's cot is from Nov 1981; guest room for Marge).
- Harry: cot in the junk room by night, playpen in the front room by day (Nov 1981 onward). Moves to the cupboard under the stairs in autumn 1982 (chapter 2).
- Books in the house (to be fixed as they appear): Ladybird Key Words readers bought for Dudley; The Very Hungry Caterpillar; Collins dictionary; a world atlas; Children's Britannica (bought Jan 1983); Petunia's cookery books; Vernon's Reader's Digest condensed novels; the Highway Code; Yellow Pages; Argos catalogue; Radio Times; the Daily Mail daily.
- Television: ordinary set until May 1982; then a Teletext set bought by Vernon "for the cricket and the share prices". Old set stands in the junk room, flex wound, facing the wall. Petunia leaves subtitles on most evenings out of habit.
- Junk room boxes labelled Xmas Decs, Dudley Baby Clothes, Marge's Things Do Not Throw. Jar of buttons and beads (now cracked, hidden). Ladybird books and a torn old catalogue in the box by the window.
- Cupboard: width of the mattress, stairs for ceiling, a spider in the high corner. Bolt outside.
- Petunia's bureau drawer in the front room: Dumbledore's 1971 reply to her, and his 1981 letter (chapter 3).

## Harry's capabilities by date
- Nov 1981: five words (Mummy, Daddy, cat, no, up). Can hold a spoken word whole and compare it. Sees scan lines, falling dust, the edge of moving light.
- Feb 1982: understands dozens of words from television and Petunia; matches some printed words from the Ladybird books to their sounds. First magic at Privet Drive: the bottle comes to him (pictured, not merely wanted).
- Apr 1982: first deliberate words, "Please" and "Water, please", in the high chair. Speaks only when it buys something. Dudley already says "won't" (canon, from before the scar night).
- Jun–Jul 1982: reading cracked from Ceefax subtitles (page found by Petunia in June). Reads the Daily Mail, cereal box, Radio Times, the Argos-type catalogue (numbers and sums from prices). Can climb out of the cot. Read all twelve Ladybird books during Dudley's party.
- Autumn 1982: wandless, at night, pictured not wanted: lifts socks, beads, pages; objects move at a slow walking pace only; a jar he did not picture landing cracked (hidden under curtains); a steady light the size of an egg over his palm that lasts while he holds the picture. Books too heavy to lift; bigger things do nothing. He does not know why.
- Oct 1982: accidental magic in sleep after dreaming of the green light: boxes stacked against the junk-room door, bulb shattered. Moved to the cupboard under the stairs that evening (cot mattress on the floor, bolt fitted on the outside by Vernon). Can slide the bolt from inside by picturing it; has not yet gone out at night.
- Dec 1982: first evening at Mrs Figg's. Said "Harry" when Figg asked his name; he does not yet know the name Potter. Read to her from a cat book (Russian Blue, then Abyssinian) when she noticed his eyes on the lines. Told her nobody taught him, his aunt reads to Dudley, the television has the words on it; told her his aunt does not know he reads. Figg: "You're two. Two and a bit." He does not yet know his own birthday. Overheard, through the kitchen wall: "He said to keep an eye on him. He never said anything about this. Dumbledore'll want to hear, Mr Tibbles. I shall have to write." Holds "Dumbledore" as a word, not yet connected to "that man".

- Jan–Feb 1983: goes out of the cupboard at night (five nights in seven, until the first bird; Vernon gets up about half past two). Reads the twenty-volume children's encyclopaedia (red, gold spines, sold at the door in January, kept in its plastic; he replaces the plastic) in a fortnight; two volumes and part of a third the first night. Light now a circle the size of a dinner plate on the page.
- READING SPEED (fixed): about 1,500 words a minute, roughly five times a good adult reader. Limit is the eye: about 8–10 fixations a second, 2–3 words each. A page takes about as long as Vernon climbing the stairs. A newspaper is an hour. Twenty encyclopaedia volumes are about 35 hours. The house's print was exhausted by the end of February 1983; after that a day's new print is an hour or two. Hogwarts library (11,000 books) would be three to four years at ten hours a day.
- Feb 1983: dictionary straight through (four nights); atlas; six Reader's Digest condensed volumes (first fiction); cookery books; telephone directory and Yellow Pages (Little Whinging has three plumbers, two solicitors, a dentist, a TV repairer, a public library on the High Street open Tue–Sat); Highway Code; car manual; Ceefax pages at night with the sound off and the door shut.
- Mar 1983: the bureau's bottom drawer, biscuit tin with a Scottish castle on the lid: marriage lines, Dudley's birth certificate, photographs of Petunia with red-haired Lily laughing, and two letters in green ink from Albus Dumbledore. 1981 letter: Lily and James Potter killed 31 Oct at Godric's Hollow by Lord Voldemort; he is not thought dead; Lily's protection holds while Harry lives under the roof of her blood; asks Petunia to take him; warns what might come if not; names him Harry James Potter, one year old on 31 July. 1971 letter to Miss Petunia Evans: kind refusal of a place at the school; hopes it will not come between the sisters. Harry now knows his name, birthday, parents' names, Voldemort's name, Godric's Hollow, and that the car crash is Petunia's story. He feels sorry for Petunia.
- Magic by summer 1983: bolt without looking; light the size of an orange, steady; pulls a thin book across the carpet along a fully pictured path; turns pages; warmth in the palms (a week of nights to get; tires him). Nothing larger moves. He does not know why.
- Summer 1983 (turned three on 31 Jul, unmarked): the election; the Thursday local paper (boy of six drowned in the canal, chip-pan fire, man who lost his job and van); unemployment number with a three in front. He finds that he minds, and that what he wants is for things to be otherwise for people; he has time nobody else has; the answer may lie with the green-ink people as much as with the plumbers. That is as far as he gets.
- Mar–Apr 1983, the empty months: nightly magic trials, one a night; took the bureau pencil into the cupboard and practised letters on newspaper margins an hour a night (for a list, not for himself); went on with sums from the encyclopaedia (primes, areas) with no way to check them; days: Radio 4 in the kitchen from breakfast to the lunchtime news; the garden.
- Apr 1983: asked Petunia for a library ticket at breakfast; refused ("he was two"). Asked Mrs Figg in the street, in Petunia's hearing, whether she had any more books; Figg offered a day a week; Tuesdays began the following week (Petunia's hair and big shop).
- Figg's shelf (cat books, green-backed detective novels on the landing, Pears Cyclopaedia by the fire) gone in two Tuesdays. Third Tuesday: his first written list, pencil on the back of an envelope, leaning letters: adult history of England, stars, money and why prices move, the body, algebra ("The encyclopaedia said there was such a thing. It didn't say what."). From early May: six library books a week on Figg's ticket from the High Street library, read by Tuesday night; the desk asked who they were for, Figg said "a boy she minded". Weekly lists; his writing improved because it had to be read. Figg asks what he made of them, waits, sometimes tells him he is wrong. She says nothing about Dumbledore or his mother; writes letters some Tuesdays and bags them.
- Jul 1983: a week-old Daily Prophet in Figg's kitchen bin (moving photograph of a man in a tall hat waving). Read every word: Ministry of Magic with a woman Minister; Azkaban; a London shop selling cauldrons; a game played in the air; and on page four, in a piece about school governors, Hogwarts and its Headmaster Albus Dumbledore. He connects the green-ink letters, Figg's "Dumbledore'll want to hear", and the Headmaster.
- Summer 1983: letter to the council about the unfenced towpath behind the recreation ground, dictated to Figg and signed by her; reply in three weeks ("under consideration"); fence went up in October 1983 (may be unrelated). Harry's first act in the world.
- Third Tuesday in August 1983: "Do you know Albus Dumbledore?" Figg: "Yes. I'll have to write to him, won't I."

- Second Tuesday of September 1983: Dumbledore at Figg's, 11am to 2pm, by the back door (Apparition sounds to Harry like a car door a street away, with a crack in the middle). Dark red suit, sherbet lemons. Harry's first words to him: "You put the lights out." Harry remembers the lights, the cat, Hagrid, and being put to sleep.
- Harry told Dumbledore he read Petunia's letters and put them back; Dumbledore: "Her letters were hers." / "I had seen to that" (nobody to ask).
- Harry's account of the speed: the television draws fifty times a second; he watches the line go down "the way you would watch somebody walk across a room" (he cannot compare it to any outside event, since those are all slowed too). Dumbledore: "Some hundreds of times, then." The number is never said aloud. To stay between Harry, Dumbledore and Figg. Harry will not tell; will not deny it if asked straight out; Dumbledore accepts.
- Dumbledore used Legilimency without asking; Harry felt pages being turned and asked; Dumbledore stopped, apologised, saw "a great deal going past very fast" and could hold none of it; will not do it again unless Harry says he may. Figg: "Albus."
- Dumbledore's answers: Voldemort not dead, whereabouts and form unknown, will try to come back, may take a long time; does not know where Harry is. The protection: Lily's death left something in Harry drawing strength from her blood; holds while Harry makes his home where Petunia does; "several weeks in every year at the least", more while he is small. Dumbledore learned of the cupboard and bolt; will speak to Petunia so nothing comes back on Harry. Harry: "I don't mind the cupboard. I mind having nothing to read."
- Harry showed the sugar bowl and the orange-sized light. Dumbledore: most children do it once or twice by accident and cannot repeat it; Harry does it when he tries; Dumbledore has a guess why big things fail and will not give a guess. Wand promised before Christmas 1983, with someone beside him until Dumbledore is satisfied. Teacher: "I will think about who."
- Parents: James reckless, loyal, good on a broomstick; Lily kind, quick, did not suffer fools; both twenty-one. More to come a little at a time. Hagrid named; "he will cry when he does" see Harry.
- Sirius: Harry described the black-haired man who held him and cried, heart going fast. Dumbledore named Sirius Black, James's closest friend since eleven, in Azkaban "because it was he who told the man with the red eyes where to find you." Harry: "He was crying." Dumbledore: "Yes. I expect he was." Harry did not know what to make of that and did not try to then. No suspicion of innocence.
- Books left on Figg's table: A History of Magic, Magical Theory, The Standard Book of Spells Grade One, Hogwarts: A History. More a few at a time. Dumbledore to return in a fortnight; wants Harry at Figg's more than Tuesdays; Figg: "She'll let him go." Dumbledore's promise: if there is something he won't tell, he'll say so rather than make something up.

- Sept 1983: Dumbledore visited Petunia on a Tuesday while Harry was at Figg's (different tobacco smell; Petunia washing cups). Bolt gone Wednesday, holes filled and painted. Petunia: Harry to Figg's every weekday, in by half past four, "not for your benefit". Nobody speaks of it.
- The four books read on the first Tuesday, reread Wednesday. Hogwarts: A History says the library holds some eleven thousand volumes. Magical Theory: wand, word, movement; nothing in it explains his wandless work. Tried "Lumos" with a knitting needle: only his own palm light came. Dumbledore's second visit (second Tuesday): a crate of the seven years' books plus staff reading and a dictionary of magical terms (about three weeks' reading). Prophet owl to Figg's from the next morning; Figg pays it; Mr Tibbles watches. Harry asked to go to the Hogwarts library: "Not while there are students in the building. In the summer, perhaps. I have not decided."
- Nov 1983: Prophet piece on the Boy Who Lived's unknown whereabouts (Godric's Hollow photo; Ministry and Dumbledore decline to comment; letters for and against). Dumbledore: everyone knows the name; most think he did it on purpose; hiding him from being looked at was one reason among several and not the chief one. "Am I [all right]?" "Better than September; better again by spring, and I am working on how."
- End Nov 1983: first visit to the High Street library (low brick building, ramp, knocking radiator). Mrs Dennison at the desk (younger than Figg, glasses on a chain) issues a ticket in Harry's name on Figg's signature; twelve books a week from then; he chooses his own.
- Second week of Dec 1983: London with Dumbledore. Knitted hat over the scar. Side-along Apparition is very bad for Harry (the squeeze lasts; he cannot breathe inside it); Dumbledore promised another way; return by Floo from the Leaky Cauldron into Figg's gas fire, long but less bad. Leaky Cauldron yard, the wall opening (he sees each brick turn). Two people say "Headmaster"; "your grandson?" "A young friend." Gringotts cart is the first thing in two years to pass him at its proper pace. Potter vault: Dumbledore holds the key, let Harry hold it; heaped gold, silver, bronze; Galleon about five pounds; Harry's estimate "about five houses in Privet Drive"; Dumbledore: comfortable, not great; he will spend on Harry's behalf and tell him. Took a bag.
- Dec 1983, Gringotts cart: Harry asked for some gold to become pounds and be put where it would grow. Dumbledore: will find out; wants reasons on paper. Harry wrote two pages in pencil that week.
- Dec 1983, same day: Charing Cross Road bookshop (five floors) with Dumbledore after Diagon Alley; forty Galleons changed; £190 of textbooks (maths at two levels, physics x3, chemistry x2, biology, blue economics, history of England x4, law, medical dictionary, statistics). Harry had asked for Muggle books on the second Tuesday in Sept 1983 and listed them in October. Monthly London trips after (Dumbledore in March 1984; Lupin from April). End Jan 1984: all sixteen-year-olds' examined material in six subjects; end Feb: into undergraduate books.
- Ollivander's: Ollivander knew him under the hat; recited Lily's and James's wands; thirty-one wands tried after the first (a vase knocked over); holly, eleven inches, phoenix feather, warm in the hand before use, red and gold sparks. Ollivander: the phoenix gave one other feather, in a wand sold long ago to a young man whose last known act with it was Harry's scar; "curious". Dumbledore's face did not move. Seven Galleons.
- Flourish and Blotts: an hour; a stack Dumbledore shrank to matchbox size. Apothecary: "Not at Arabella's." "Where, then." "We shall see."
- Jan–Feb 1984 trials, twenty of each a night at Figg's table, counts kept in his head: wand+word, feather and book every time; wand without word, feather 17/20 lower, book 6/20; word without wand, nothing; picture alone, feather every time at walking pace, book never; wandless speed cannot be increased by seeing it faster; with the wand, as fast as he sees it up to a ceiling he cannot push past. He does not know what the numbers mean; keeps them for whoever will. Learned the chore charms (page-turning, ink-drying, fetching, a light that stays). Observed: a charm for every chore, none for most of what is wrong with people's lives.
- Wand work at Figg's, Dec 1983–Feb 1984: Lumos first evening (white, steady, at the wand tip, costs nothing; held an hour). Wand is longer than his forearm; the swish and flick defeated his grip until January (an hour a day practice). Thursday in January: feather rises fast; then the Standard Book rises (first thing heavier than a sock ever to move for him). Then: wand, movement, picture, no word: the feather rose, lower and slower. Filed, not pursued. By February: all first-year charms, starting second.

- Mar 1984, a Thursday evening: McGonagall by Floo into Figg's gas fire (cats shut in the kitchen). Questioned him on theory; told him the book's mass explanation is "not quite right" and why. Match to needle in one pass, no incantation, twice; she kept the needles. "That is not a first attempt." Harry told her he can see all of a thing for as long as he wants and that the Headmaster asked him to keep the how to three people; she did not ask. Her conditions: nothing living, nothing on himself or anyone, nothing he has not read the whole chapter on including what goes wrong; break them and she does not come back. Fortnightly Thursdays at that hour. Read fourth and fifth year.
- Dumbledore told Harry about Lupin a week ahead: friend of James and Lily, one of three (the others dead or in Azkaban), 24, poor, bitten as a small boy; absent the day of the moon and the day after; Figg agreed.
- First Tuesday in Mar 1984: Dumbledore's answer on the gold. Goblins allow gold out as pounds if it does not return as gold; charged a fee. Mr Hadley (Muggle-born, Hogwarts some years after Lupin, solicitor) holds £10,000 (2,000 Galleons, a tenth of the vault) in trust from April 1984; Harry's instructions: large dividend-paying companies, dividends reinvested, nothing sold without a written reason. Hadley explained trusts for a quarter of an hour; one question to look up. Harry: if it grows, he wants half the vault; Dumbledore: with figures. "You might have left out the part where you explained to me what a bank was."
- Apr 1984, a Monday: Lupin arrives by the back door. Muggle overcoat with the hem gone, mended robes. Sits on the floor. "You were sick on me." Answered Harry's list all morning: Lily went quiet and used full names when angry; James played Quidditch alone in the garden on Sundays and read the paper aloud doing voices; both read a great deal; Lily liked Petunia, stopped trying to mend it about a year before she died and minded; James afraid of very little; there was a cat with a stupid name Lupin could not recall. Harry did nothing else while Lupin talked, for the first time in his life.
- Lupin weekdays except around the moon. From the second week he keeps Harry's trial counts on paper with a watch Dumbledore lent. Observation: Harry gets a spell right first time if he can see what it does, wrong when the movement needs a bigger hand, right a fortnight later. Lupin teaches what books leave out (Filch, Howlers, how the Ministry works, how people get jobs). Took Harry to the library when Figg's knees were bad; Mrs Dennison had been told to expect him.
- Oct 1983: read Figg's Kneazle correspondence with permission; one letter in four mentions a Muggle object. Kept against the need for hands.
- Second Tuesday in Apr 1984: proposed the sheet to Figg (pens, torches with batteries, tape, flasks, lined notebooks; nothing with a plug, nothing enchanted; Lupin checked the law). Written agreement in pencil, signed by Figg, sat on by Mr Tibbles. First sheet with eleven letters, third week of April: pen 2 Sickles (cost 12p), torch 1 Galleon with batteries, tape 1 Sickle; nine orders, one for twelve pens. By June every customer and their friends; Lupin shops Monday mornings and wraps Monday afternoons; Galleons in a biscuit tin; 40 Galleons taken in May.
- May 1984: Lupin's two paid research afternoons a week begin (Dumbledore told what is asked). First report, second week of May: register 211 names (believed by nobody informed; Lupin not on it); Prophet owned by the Cuffe family for a century; shop rent 40 Galleons a month at the wrong end of Diagon Alley, 200 at the right. Harry does not believe the 211.
- Jun 1984: revision book planned (eleven bad ones read in London in March); 200 pages in pencil would be seventy hours; waits for the ink.
- Early May 1984: Wolfsbane. Lupin: a month costs more than he earns in three. Harry: "I've got five houses of money and nothing to spend it on but books"; "It's what the money is for." Lupin refused (Harry is three; James offered the same and was refused). Dumbledore approved; drawn up as part of Lupin's wage. Flask by owl from a London apothecary a week before the May moon; Lupin back on the Thursday less grey. Nobody spoke of it.
- May–Jun 1984: the quill. Levitation-steered quill writes well but at hand pace; a whole word at once gains a little. Levitation plus the third-year path-moving charm: wrote the first letter repeatedly until the nib went through the paper and scored the table; a hole the shape of a capital D in Figg's tablecloth (Harry paid from the vault). Lupin: everybody tries it at school, two charms pull against each other; some makers do it on purpose and it takes years. Harry's letters to Dumbledore are weekly, an hour each in pencil.
- Jun 1984: ink moved directly. Saw the ink leave the bottle in a thread and lie down as his name; first result a penny-sized blot with a tail. Learned to see the ink as ink (spreading, running, wanting to be a drop) as well as the letters. By end of the afternoon one blurred heavy word, "Harry", arriving as fast as the bead crossed the room; slower than pencil overall because he must look at the result before seeing the next word. Lupin: stick to the pencil for now.
- Lupin's observation corrected: hand size is the limit; some movements will not be possible for years whatever he does. Practice fixes technique, not reach.
- Last Tuesday in June 1984: the Dursleys have agreed. First three weeks of July at Privet Drive all day every day (Dumbledore's condition). Last week of July Lupin collects him for Hogwarts until September: a room, meals with staff, the library except behind the grille. Madam Pince warned and not pleased. Harry's sum: the library is three to four years at his pace. "It's a long time."

- 1–21 Jul 1984 at Privet Drive. Dudley (four) has Harry fetch and read to him ("He does the voices"). Vernon watches, says nothing. Harry practises wand movements without casting (company condition). Petunia ill two days; Harry made breakfasts and began teaching Dudley his letters (P, D, H by day three); Petunia saw and did not stop it. Petunia's one speech: "You're not to do anything in this house. Not in front of him. Whatever you do at hers." Harry: "I won't."
- Mon 23 Jul 1984: Knight Bus from Wisteria Walk with Lupin (eleven Sickles; conductor noted the hat). Harry not sick. Set down on the road above the lake in early afternoon. Hagrid at the gate, wept; sat on the grass; pumpkins on Tuesdays and Fridays.
- Hogwarts, summer 1984: Harry's room on the second floor two doors from the hospital wing, window over the lake. Lupin along the corridor. Pomfrey's check: ten hours' sleep; Harry asked whether it must be ten; Pomfrey: yes, body mends and brain files in sleep, no potion for it, "ask me again when you're grown"; Harry: "I will." Looked at the scar; spoke to Dumbledore in the corridor. Staff table of eight: Dumbledore, McGonagall (often away), Flitwick, Sprout, Pomfrey, Pince (silent at meals), Hagrid, Lupin; Snape from the third evening, then rarely.
- Library (fourth floor). Madam Pince read Dumbledore's letter twice. Rules: no book leaves without her stamp; no food, drink or ink; the table nearest her desk; books returned to her, not the shelf; one turned-down corner and he is out. Ledger is by year of acquisition; no subject list. Harry's reading order by usefulness: Charms, Potions, Healing, law and Ministry publications, history; Divination last after one book. Four books the first afternoon. Third day: offered Pince a subject card index; she refused ink in the library; he writes cards in his room from memory. By end of summer 390 cards in a shoebox on her desk (title, shelf, six lines on content, clerk's hand); she used one to answer Sprout in mid-August. PENDING: catalogue continues each visit. By mid-August she leaves books on his table unasked.
- Snape: "Why does it matter which way you stir?" / "Because the brew is not a soup." Allowed the second-year room: touch nothing of his, Lupin present when there is a fire, accidents reported by Lupin. Named Borage, third chapter; Harry read it by nine next morning. Three more dinners, one question each, a book each time; the third question was whether Wolfsbane can be brewed in large batches: Belby has not tried, nobody has asked, not a stupid question, no book. Sprout: two ingredients dear because rare, three because one London apothecary is the only stockist; she has told the Ministry twice. Last week of August: "You ask things the way your mother did. As if you were owed the answer." / "She usually was."
- Flitwick: sat on the library floor; answered three questions about the library's charms (book from Pince's desk, eighty years old). Harry told him the limit is his eyes, not understanding. Flitwick: Omniocular charm is a cousin of the photograph charm with a rate; you'd want words given one at a time where the eye already is; knows nothing that does it, knows nothing that says it can't. No answer by end of summer; keeps coming back.
- 31 Jul 1984, fourth birthday, first one marked: Hagrid's treacle cake; Figg's card (cat; her name and the cats' names); Dumbledore's own school book with his marginal notes; McGonagall's matches and note; Flitwick's gutted Omniocular ("for looking at"); Lupin's photograph of James laughing, Lily looking from pushchair to James and back, Harry under a sun hat, four seconds looping. Kept in Harry's shirt pocket.
- Ink at Hogwarts, evenings in his room (Pince's no-ink rule does not reach there). End of July: a line at a time, arriving whole. Mid-August: a paragraph. Letters heavy, edges run ("a note written in a moving carriage", Lupin). Weekly letter to Dumbledore down from an hour in pencil to about a quarter of an hour. Writes to Figg and to Mrs Dennison the same way. Wrote to Bathilda Bagshot (Godric's Hollow) with a page of questions on what the History left out; nine-page reply in a bad hand, four answers, eleven new questions, knew his grandparents, invites him to visit when someone can bring him. PENDING: the visit.
- Summer 1984: Figg kept the owls going at a quarter pace, Lupin's shopping done in advance; weekly notes on the Prophet wrapper; Harry answered in ink.
- Daily shape, Aug 1984: up at six; library seven to eleven; dungeons with Lupin eleven to one (second-year brews); library to dusk; Hagrid Tue and Fri; Dumbledore's office some evenings (Fawkes on his perch, portraits). Real numbers: a book in an hour and ten minutes; library seven to seven; eleven thousand books is a little under four years with terms out, finishing at eight.

- 1 Sep 1984: moves to Figg's back bedroom (window, shelf; Tufty's bed). Sleeps there weekdays and Saturdays; Sundays at Privet Drive after breakfast until tea, walked round by Lupin. Cupboard is a cupboard again. Dudley's letters continue on Sundays (has P, D, H, O).
- Sep 1984 list of eight for Christmas: the book; hands; a student; the trust; the potion; a brewer's name; children; the reading device.
- Sun 2 Sep 1984: wrote the mathematics revision book in ink in a day (200 pages, places people go wrong marked); Lupin wished he had had it at fifteen; to Hadley on the Tuesday. Chemistry the next Sunday, physics the one after. Nov 1984: Oxford educational publisher paid £600 outright, no author's name; Harry took it against Hadley's advice. Trust up a little under a tenth by Nov; Harry will ask for half the vault in spring with reasons, Hadley to see them first.
- Sep–Oct 1984: Hester Pye hired (left Hogwarts June 1984 with seven passes; refused by Ministry twice and three shops; parents keep a newsagent's in Luton); 12 Galleons a week; told she works for Harry though Figg's name is on the letters. Owls move to the spare room above the Luton newsagent's by end Oct. Gringotts account in Figg's name: 320 Galleons end Oct after wages, 510 by Christmas. Hester raised the pen price by a Sickle; orders held.
- Nov 1984: Colin (younger brother of Hadley's clerk, engineering at Imperial, short of money) fetches library books and copies journals on Fridays for £20 a week; brings unrequested things that are usually right. By Christmas about a third of a degree's reading in each of six subjects. Chemistry read against Borage: agree about heat, nothing else; kept for Snape. Statistics applied to his own counts. Lupin reading second-year economics.
- Autumn 1984 Lupin reports: apothecaries buy from three growers and a Dorset breeder; Honeydukes' sugar from a Leeds Muggle wholesaler via a Squib with a van; register of commercial brewers has nine names, youngest sixty.
- Third Saturday in Oct 1984: Godric's Hollow by Knight Bus with Lupin. The obelisk becomes the statue (James, Lily, baby without scar) for wizards. The house: upper right storey open, ivy, roof gone; sign in gold letters with messages in many hands (one in green: "not alone"). Harry did not go in. The grave: white marble; Harry asked about the epitaph; Lupin never understood it either; Lupin talks to the grave sometimes. Harry wants to come back.
