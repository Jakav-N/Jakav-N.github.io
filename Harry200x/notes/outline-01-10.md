# Outline: chapters 1–10 (October 1981 to August 1985)

Each chapter about 3,000 words. Close third on Harry. Dates in real time. Subjective durations are never attached to decisions.

## Chapter 1 — 31 Oct 1981 to Feb 1982 (15 to 18 months)
- Godric's Hollow from inside the cot, restrained and non-graphic: the door, Lily, the man with red eyes, green light, pain, the room coming apart, and the change. Dust that keeps falling. His own cry stretched into a long low sound; he stops because it frightens him. His mother on the floor; he waits.
- Hagrid (huge, weeping). Sirius (black hair, white face; holds Harry against his chest; Harry keeps the face for life, which matters in 1987–88). The flight: wind that presses and stays; stars that slide. Privet Drive: Dumbledore, McGonagall becoming a cat (he watches every stage; the seed of his later Transfiguration), the Put-Outer, a sleep charm so the night on the step passes.
- Petunia's scream in slow motion; she reads the letter; her face closes.
- First weeks: cot in the junk room at night, playpen in the front room by day. Perception: the crack in the ceiling and the moving edge of light; sounds as long shapes he must hold whole; Dudley's thrown toys whose landing he can predict and not prevent; his own arm is as slow as everything else. He understands that he has sped up and nothing else has. No words for it; he has five words and sets about getting more.
- Television: the scan line and the trail of light; the dot when it is switched off. Sound is the useful part: "today", "news", "weather", "rain" (the first word he is sure of). Petunia's naming-talk to Dudley. The Ladybird Key Words books read aloud facing his corner; he begins matching shapes to sounds. It takes the winter.
- Petunia on the telephone to Marge: "her boy", "car crash". He learns he is a category, and that there is a story about his parents told in a lowered voice.
- The bottle: hungry, Petunia upstairs, he pictures the bottle coming and it comes, at walking pace. Petunia sees. Vernon that evening in the kitchen: "one of them", "that man said we had to keep him", "I won't have it in my house". He concludes: this is the thing they fear; it belongs to his mother's side; "that man" has some hold over them; he will not do it where they can see.
- Ends on the ordinary sounds of the house at night, which he now knows one from another.

## Chapter 2 — Mar to Dec 1982 (19 months to 2 years 5 months)
- Speech. He understands most of what is said by spring. Speaking is muscle; he practises in the cot at night, in real time, like an instrument. First deliberate words chosen for effect on Petunia: "please", "thank you", "water, please". Petunia is unsettled ("he's doing it on purpose"); Vernon: "showing off". He learns competence is unwelcome here and talks less; talking is slow anyway.
- The Falklands on the news and in the Daily Mail. First real contact with death as a thing that happens to other people's parents. The "car crash" story filed, not yet doubted.
- Reading: Vernon buys a Teletext set (for share prices and the cricket). Petunia uses the Ceefax subtitles when Dudley is screaming. Paired text and speech cracks reading in weeks. Then the Ladybird books, the Argos catalogue (pictures, words, prices: his first arithmetic), the Radio Times, the Daily Mail, cereal boxes. Fluent by his second birthday, which nobody marks.
- Magic, deliberately: he reproduces the bottle. Wanting does nothing; picturing the end state does. A sock, a bead, a page. A small light over his palm when he wants to read in the dark; it is the thing he most needs. Learns that an incomplete picture produces a mess (the glass that cracks).
- The slip: Petunia sees pages turning while his hands are on the bars. The cupboard under the stairs, "where we can shut the door". His assessment: dark (he has light), quiet, no television (bad), and at night the house is his. He opens the bolt from inside the first night by picturing it sliding.
- Mrs Figg babysits for the first time (Grunnings dinner, November). Her house: cabbage, cats who stare at him, photographs of cats. She asks his name; he says "Harry Potter" and watches her go still. She gives him a cat book; he reads it; she sees. To Mr Tibbles, assuming he cannot follow: "Dumbledore'll want to hear about this." He adds the name to what he knows: "that lot", "that man", "the letter", "Dumbledore".

## Chapter 3 — Jan to Aug 1983 (2 years 5 months to 3)
- Nights in the house. Children's Britannica bought for Dudley from a door-to-door salesman; the Collins dictionary; the atlas; the Highway Code; Petunia's cookery books; Yellow Pages; Vernon's Reader's Digest condensed novels (his first fiction; he likes stories). The Open University on BBC2 in the small hours with the sound low.
- The bureau drawer: Dumbledore's 1971 reply to an eleven-year-old Petunia and the letter from 1981. He learns his parents' names, how they died, the name of the man, the school, the protection that holds while he lives in this house, and that his aunt once asked to be let in. He sits on the carpet at three in the morning with the letters under his light. Petunia becomes someone he understands and is sorry for.
- Magic practice nightly: the bolt, a steadier light, page-turning, lifting a volume of the encyclopaedia, pulling an object across the room, warming his hands in January. The limits: fine control is hard; big things do not move; nothing comes from nothing. Picturing precisely matters more than wanting.
- What he wants. Specific observations over the year: the news (unemployment, the election, the first famine reports), Petunia's fierceness for Dudley, Figg with her cats, his mother between the man and the cot. He concludes that what matters to him is that things go well for people, and that he is unusually placed to make them go well. One page, from particulars, no sermon.
- Figg's Tuesdays become regular (Petunia's shop and hair). Figg talks to him. He finds a Daily Prophet in her kitchen bin, with moving photographs, and reads it. In August he asks her: "Do you know Albus Dumbledore?" She puts her cup down. "I'll have to write to him, won't I."

## Chapter 4 — Sep to Oct 1983 (3 years 1 month)
- Dumbledore at Figg's on a Tuesday. He expects a precocious toddler and meets a person. Harry answers how he learned to read: "Everything you do is slow to me. I've been awake a very long time." Dumbledore's gentle look and Harry's "What was that?" Dumbledore tells him (Legilimency), apologises, does not do it again. Trust begins there.
- Dumbledore explains the protection in Lily's blood and Petunia's house, and that it needs Harry to regard the house as home. They settle terms: at least three weeks a year at Privet Drive. He tells Harry what he believes about Voldemort (not dead), and about James and Lily, plainly.
- Harry asks for books, a wand, people who can teach him, and the truth. Dumbledore agrees to books at once and a wand soon with supervision. Harry lifts the sugar bowl and lights his palm; Dumbledore is quiet for a while. The speed stays between the three of them. Harry asks that Petunia be told nothing new. Dumbledore mentions the Ministry will not know of his magic until his letter.
- The first books on Figg's table: A History of Magic, Magical Theory, The Standard Book of Spells Grade 1, Fantastic Beasts, Hogwarts: A History, One Thousand Magical Herbs and Fungi, Magical Drafts and Potions. The Prophet to be delivered to Figg's.

## Chapter 5 — Nov 1983 to Feb 1984 (3 years 3 months to 3 years 6 months)
- A book a day, then the Prophet daily. The wizarding world as it looks from print: tiny, old, ranked, frightened of a name. He estimates its size from the Hogwarts intake.
- Little Whinging public library with Figg weekly. The librarian (plain name, to be chosen) lets him into the adult shelves after he reads her the back of a novel. Maximum books on Figg's ticket.
- Diagon Alley with Dumbledore in December: the Leaky Cauldron's back yard, the Alley in winter, Gringotts and the Potter vault (more than he expected and less than it looks), Ollivander's (holly and phoenix feather, eleven inches; "curious"), Flourish and Blotts by the box, the apothecary. Hat over the scar; nobody looks at a child beside Dumbledore.
- First wand work at Figg's: Lumos cleaner than his palm-light; Wingardium Leviosa fails because the movement is written for an adult arm and the wand is longer than his forearm. Motor practice in real time. He finds the incantation can be dropped once the picture is good; the movement less so. Wandless remains easier for small things; the wand gives power and precision.

## Chapter 6 — Mar to Jun 1984 (3 years 7 months to 3 years 11 months)
- McGonagall's visit at Dumbledore's request. Dry questioning on theory. The matchstick becomes a needle in one pass because he has seen a woman become a cat at a speed where every stage was visible and he pictures the whole needle. She sits down. Monthly lessons agreed.
- Lupin recruited. Dumbledore tells Harry about the lycanthropy beforehand; Lupin tells him again himself. Thin, patched, twenty-four. He knew James and Lily and answers questions for an afternoon. Wolfsbane: what it costs. Harry pays from the vault, framed as a tutor's wage so that it is Lupin's. First money he spends on someone.
- Lupin daily at Figg's. How wizards actually live. Grade 1 to 3 charms hampered by hand size. First attempt to chain spells to make a quill write: it writes the first letter until the ink runs out. The lesson: magic interprets intent, and chained spells carry context he did not mean to pass.

## Chapter 7 — Jul to Aug 1984 (4)
- Three weeks at Privet Drive first. Dudley at four; Petunia's wariness; Vernon's complaints; Harry decent to all of them.
- Hogwarts in summer. The castle at his speed (brushstrokes in portraits rearranging; the staircases). Hagrid, who cries. Madam Pince and the letter from the Headmaster she reads twice; 11,000 books; the arithmetic of four to six a day. Pomfrey's check (scar, general health, sleeps normally). Snape, who stays over summer: cold to Lupin, cold to Harry; Harry's questions about brewing are good ones; Snape allows the second-year classroom under Lupin's eye.
- Flitwick, delighted, on how the library's charms work and how Omnioculars replay. Harry files that as the direction for faster reading. Dumbledore's office; Fawkes.

## Chapter 8 — Sep to Dec 1984 (4 years 1 month to 4 years 5 months)
- Moves to Figg's full time; Petunia relieved; his belongings in one box.
- London with Lupin: Foyles and Dillons, boxes of textbooks in mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, economics, law, history, bought with exchanged gold. Five books a day through the autumn. The Financial Times at Figg's. He works out the wizarding economy is minuscule and that the muggle one is where scale is, and that a four-year-old has no legal hands to use there yet.
- Christmas at Hogwarts with the handful of students who stay. A fifth-year sees him; letters home; the first rumour.
- McGonagall monthly: Transfiguration surges because it is intent-driven. Fourth-year object work by December.

## Chapter 9 — Jan to Apr 1985 (4 years 5 months to 4 years 9 months)
- The output problem. Dead ends: dictation quills (slow), copying charms (need a source object), steering an animated quill wandlessly (capped near twice normal casting speed). Then McGonagall's point that the end state must be held entire in the mind, and the observation that a page of writing is a shape. He transfigures a blank page into a written one by picturing it. A letter to Dumbledore is the first page. McGonagall's reaction. Limits: one page per cast, about two seconds each, errors where the picture is imprecise.
- He writes for others: letters, notes for Lupin's lessons, and a first manuscript, a plain primer on magical theory for first-years, which Dumbledore reads.
- The Prophet, from a student's letter home: Dumbledore confirms Harry Potter is well, is in his care part of the time, and is advanced for his age. Letters start arriving by owl.

## Chapter 10 — May to Aug 1985 (4 years 10 months to 5)
- The letters: Augusta Longbottom (Neville, same age); Molly Weasley (a jumper, a summer invitation); Lucius Malfoy (smooth; answered honestly and declined); the Prophet (interview declined); hundreds from ordinary people, all answered a page at a time.
- Neville and Augusta at Hogwarts in June. Neville's fear of his grandmother and doubt of his own magic. Harry shows him the small wandless things; a pebble lifts an inch; Augusta's face.
- Three weeks at Privet Drive in July. Fifth birthday: nothing from the Dursleys, a cake from Figg, owls at the window, Dudley curious about owls. A concrete small kindness to Dudley.
- First attempt at spell creation: binding the page method to an incantation and movement so Lupin can use it. Weeks of attempts; the binding does not take. He understands "connection" is not a metaphor and sets the problem aside with its failure recorded for Dumbledore. The Burrow invitation accepted for August.
