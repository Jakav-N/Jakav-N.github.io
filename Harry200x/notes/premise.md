# Premise (Jacob's text, verbatim)

# Magic system
- Inventing new spell primitives is not possible (obviously nobody knows this for certain, but no spell has been made since people can remember)
- Same for potions: nobody's can invent new primitives, but some work can be done.
- There are a lot of practically useful spells that do not come up in canon. To think of what these might be, think about time limitations or other limitations.
- There are several types of magic: spells, potions, etc, and the "accidental" kind, which typically only happen with young children. Harry will not lose this capability over time. Spells can be combined kinda like programming, but this is very rarely done. Spells can be attached to objects, places, or people (among other things, these are just some things magic can reference); this is warding. It is easier to attach spells to places and objects than people. There are also rituals, similar to spells and potions, but rarely come up.
- Spell invention is binding an incantation and wand movement to a spell. It is very difficult, requires some connection between the effect and what you are binding it to.
- Spell creation is dangerous in the same way that programming is: your spells can easily have bugs if at all complicated
- About 10% of commonly used spells were invented
- All primitives cannot create food, gold, or reverse aging: this is gamp's law.
- Alchemy can bypass gamp's law, but is but is nigh impossible.
- Dementors are creatures of pain and suffering, feeding off the soul. This usually cannot cause permanent damage without direct contact in the way in the canon books.
- Expecto patronum can be shaped by the right intent and thought to destroy dementors, if a person is truly good, beyond almost all, and is centered on elimination of evil, and increase of good. Normally, when using a happy memory, it is a shield.
- Spells and potions are also affected by intent and the mind. This includes transfiguration (no incantation or wand movement, although a wand is used), and expecto patronum
- wandless magic, non-verbal magic, etc is typically done by visualizing something well enough that magic interprets it as a spell. Because of this, Harry will be able to do better at it than other people, once he learns. Unfortunately, beyond a certain speed, magic will not interpret things as spells, so Harry's wandless magic can only be performed about twice as fast as the fastest normal spell casting. There are also some negative effects on spell performance
- The trace (underage magic detector), is tied with the hogwarts letter

# The speed
- His nervous system is 200x speed. It's caused by Lily's protection combined with Voldemort's attack, in a way that cannot be replicated. It impacts his brain only, so while his reaction time is lower it isn't quite superhuman.
- Eyes are unfortunately limited by saccade speed
- This is the only unrealistic thing. When the story starts, everything is the same as canon

# Harry's goals
+ From the age of 3, Harry wants to do good. He's come to the conclusion that this is what matters to him.
+ Harry will become ethical very quickly. He might not honor laws, believing them to be bad but he will not lie, steal without at minimum sufficient restitution, kill, or harm people in general.
+ Realistically, he's gonna be inventing stuff because he's so fast. Magitech? Destroying dementors? Many more things are possible, more crazy things. Many applications of muggle ideas to the wizarding world, including arbitrage, muggle-style loans, computing, engineering in a lot of ways, theory development, muggle ideas of experimentation, etc.
+ Harry is roughly a Bayesian. He understands that Bayesian methods are not practical unfortunately often, but are theoretically optional. He understands how to form accurate beliefs. This is not really talked about; unfortunately you'd likely handle this wrong if it was. Harry understands statistics very well. Again, this is rarely talked about. It's just something that Harry uses somewhat often.
+ Harry will want to be able to read a lot. By about 4.5, he'll be able to, whether still all in the Dursleys house for most the time, or not (he's somewhat likely to leave for obvious reasons)
+ Harry will want a wand. Without one, you can't really learn spells.
+ He'll also want magical books in large quantities.
+ Harry might take a while to work out magic. He'll find out how to contact the magical community somehow before Hogwarts
+ As a very young child, he will try to figure out speech and reading. He can't just magically learn (plus children are input constrained), but once he notices subtitles on the TV, he'll be reading everything rather quickly
+ Harry will want money, friends both magical and muggle, and influence because it is useful even though he doesn't especially like it.
+ Harry will do a lot of magical research. When he can outsource things, he will. So, he'll have many researchers working for him. Muggle research has potential as well but is relatively saturated, so it is more difficult.
+ Muggle and magical businesses for money.
+ Because he thinks fast, he'll probably want these things the moment he's learned enough to want them.
+ Not reveal exactly how fast he thinks to the general public to avoid creeping people out, while not hiding his capabilities in general because that would be too costly
