# Research, inventions and businesses

What Harry knows, what he does with it, when, who does the work, what it earns, and what goes wrong. This is the programme the chapters draw on. Nothing here is small, because nothing stops him.

## What he knows, and from when

- **August 1986: every book in the Hogwarts general collection**, exactly. Eleven thousand volumes: seven years of every subject taught, plus everything the school has shelved since 1000 AD that is not behind the grille. That is every charm anyone has written down, including the ones nobody has cast in three centuries; every potion recipe in print; every account of a ward; every history, law, creature, plant, and biography; the Ministry's own publications; back numbers of every journal. He can recite any page. He is the only person alive who has read it all, and the only one who can see where the books contradict each other, which they do constantly.
- **The Restricted Section**: not read. Dumbledore's line. He knows the titles from Pince's ledger and what other books say about them.
- **Muggle**: A-levels in six subjects by January 1984; undergraduate in six by autumn 1984; by 1986 roughly a degree and beyond in mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology and medicine, economics, law and history, with journals from Colin; by 1988 engineering, statistics, linguistics (Latin and Greek read, not spoken), psychology, accountancy, and whatever a problem needed. He uses it as a working engineer uses a handbook: silently.
- **His own numbers**: a quarter of a million recorded casts by 1988, his own and forty others'; nobody else has a hundred.

## The method of the house (never explained in the prose)

Briefs written by Harry; people who read, count, brew, cast, and build; a tester who is not the inventor; inert targets; written failures. He reads every report. The house is the first place in wizarding Britain where anybody writes down what did not work.

## The programme, by year

### 1984–85: the obvious things
- **Ink** (output). **The line and the word** (input, with Flitwick). **Wolfsbane by the batch** (Tilley). **Muggle goods by owl; the lending library; Muggle herbs** (the apothecaries sell ordinary plants at ten times a seed merchant's price). **Pepperup and the common potions at cost plus a tenth.** **Revision books; the postal course** (Muggle). **The subject catalogue** (Pince).
- Earnings 1985: about 5,000 Galleons after wages; the shop, the potions, the library. Muggle: about £8,000 from books and the course's first year.

### 1986: what eleven thousand books are for
- **The list of lost charms.** The first thing he does with the finished library, in August 1986, is write, in a week, a list of two hundred and forty charms and enchantments described in the collection that nobody teaches or sells, with the book and page for each and a line on what it would be good for now. Flitwick reads it in one sitting and does not sleep. Forty of them are practical and simple. The house's charms people spend 1986–87 casting them one at a time on inert targets and finding out which still work as written, which need the incantation corrected (he corrects them; he knows the Latin and the drift), and which were nonsense. Twenty-six work.
- From the list, that year: a **preserving charm** (1611) that keeps food and potions from spoiling for a month, applied to every flask the house sells; a **water-clearing charm** (1703) that makes any water drinkable, which the house gives away to anyone who asks, in a pamphlet with the incantation and the movement, because the Statute does not say a wizard may not pour clean water for a Muggle, and Harry has read the Statute; a **tally charm** (1588) that counts what passes a point, put on the shop's door and then on every cauldron; a **heat-holding charm**, rebuilt from a 1640 hearth-charm and Snape's thermometer, that holds a cauldron at a named temperature until told otherwise. The heat-holding charm goes on sale in 1987 as a stamped brass ring that sits under a cauldron, at three Galleons, and every brewer in Britain buys one, and it is the first thing the house makes that the apothecaries buy too.
- **Cauldron blanks from Sheffield** at half price. **The index card.** **The child-proof stopper**: a stopper that will not turn for a hand below a certain span, from a 1750 cellar-charm; sold with every potion from 1986, and St Mungo's asks for them in 1987.
- **Warding**: the warding reader's survey of everything in print, done by spring 1986; the conclusion that nobody has added to the subject since 1700 but names; the Gringotts door drawn; the first product in 1987.
- **Loans** at Muggle interest to Muggle-born leavers; eleven by 1988.
- Earnings 1986: about 18,000 Galleons after wages; a second shop in Hogsmeade in October. Muggle: the course at 2,000 pupils; the publisher a quarter owned.

### 1987: the house has things to sell
- **The heat ring** (above). **A ward kit**: a set of six household charms from the survey, written plain, with a card that tells the householder when one has been crossed; sold at five Galleons; the first thing most wizarding households have ever had that a Muggle would call an alarm. **A self-totalling ledger** (a 1520 counting-house charm, corrected) for shops, which Hester uses first and the Alley buys. **Automated brewing**: a Muggle engineer, Mr Adeyemi, who worked at a brewery in Burton and answered a brief, designs a rig of paddles and a fire-box that the heat ring and a 1600 stirring-charm run together; it brews Pepperup unattended in the Alley room from June 1987, under a tally charm, and St Mungo's buys from it by August at a third of what it paid.
- **The linked cards**: the pair works in November 1987.
- **The weekly paper** from September, on a press charmed to set its own type from ink laid on a master page, which is the ink-moving trick taught to a machine.
- **The first bound spell**: "next", for the cards, in the summer; the incantation chosen from the etymology of the lost charms, which is where the linguistics goes.
- **The journal** (quarterly) and the **theory of runs**, which predicts and is not talked about.
- **St Mungo's**: records on the Spell Damage ward since 1986; a ward-cleaning regime from the Muggle books, which the Healers resist and the matron adopts; the stoppers.
- **Logistics**: a card at every owl post office in the country, from 1988, that tells the house where a parcel is; the owls themselves cannot be hurried, but the knowing can.
- Earnings 1987: about 45,000 Galleons after wages; the building bought; a hundred people. Muggle: the publisher majority owned; the fund opened; the course at 4,000.

### 1988: the cards
- **The cards on sale** in September at a Galleon a pair; ten thousand pairs by Christmas; every Ministry department has them by spring; the Prophet buys two hundred and says nothing. The weekly by card from January 1989. A **card that only shows to its owner's hand**, from the stopper's charm, for anyone who asks, so that a card cannot be read by whoever holds its twin; the Ministry buys those first.
- **Automated brewing** extended to the common hospital potions; the house supplies St Mungo's entire stock of Pepperup, Skele-Gro, Blood-Replenishing and burn paste by the end of the year at a third of the old cost, and the hospital's governors write to thank Miss Pye.
- **The Patronus** studied with the Unspeakables from 1989 on Harry's conditions.
- **Fire**: a household charm from the list that smothers a fire in a room when the room is empty; sold in the ward kit from 1988; the first house it saves is in Hogsmeade in February 1989 and the weekly prints the householder's letter.
- **Dragon pox**: the house pays a Healer at St Mungo's to count cases by village for two years; the count shows what everyone suspected and nobody had written down, which is that it comes in from one market; the market is cleaned; the count falls.
- Earnings 1988: about 110,000 Galleons after wages; the largest commercial enterprise in wizarding Britain after Gringotts, which the Prophet prints before the weekly does. Muggle: the fund up a quarter in its first year; two London houses.

### 1989–91: scale
- **The index** in the Hogwarts library (1989) and then in every house that wants one. **A card that records speech** for an hour and gives it back, from the Omniocular charm and the cards, for the Wizengamot's clerks, who have never had a record. **The Floo**: a survey of how it is routed, which nobody has done, and a map, which the Ministry asks for and is given. **Broom safety**: a cushioning charm reworked for the house's brooms, which are the first cheap ones. **The course** in French and German by 1990 through Muggle-born translators.
- **Sleep**: nothing. **Time**: the pamphlet, 1989; refused.
- Earnings 1989: about 160,000 Galleons; 1990: 220,000; 1991: 280,000.

## Wealth, corrected (Jacob: he gets rich, in both economies, as a means)

He keeps the capital and gives from the income, and the giving is sized by what the causes can use, not by a fraction. The wizarding causes are small because the world is: Wolfsbane for the few hundred who cannot pay what it costs is a few thousand Galleons a year once the price is a tenth of Belby's; the paper loses about ten thousand a year by design; the course's marking is a few thousand; the loans come back; St Mungo's buys, it is not given to. By 1988 the whole list is under thirty thousand Galleons a year, and it does not grow with the income. The house's own growth takes what growth can use. The rest is the point: from 1987 the surplus is changed into pounds at Gringotts every quarter, which the goblins permit because it leaves as pounds, and goes to the Muggle side, where a Galleon's worth of pounds does more good than a Galleon can in a world that has nothing left to buy. Some builds the Oxford company. Most goes to the well-digging charity and, from 1989, to a vaccination programme Hadley finds, chosen the way the charity was chosen. In 1988 the Prophet notices Galleons leaving the wizarding economy for Muggle wells and calls it the Potter drain; the weekly prints the figure and where it went; nobody can find a law against it; the Ministry minds and says nothing. He owns, through Hester's name until 1991 and his own after: the building at the wrong end of the Alley (bought outright 1987), the shops in the Alley and Hogsmeade, the brewing house, half the Hogsmeade card workshop (all of it from 1989), the press, the Luton buildings. Gringotts holds the house's balance; a goblin director asks to meet him in 1989, because the house is the bank's largest commercial depositor, and he goes, in a hat, and they talk about nothing but rates for an hour and understand each other.

**Muggle.** Pushed, not drifted. The GCSE replaces O-levels in 1988 and every revision book in England goes out of date at once; the Oxford company, which he owns, has his sixteen subjects rewritten for the new syllabus in the spring of 1987 before most publishers have read it, and prints two hundred thousand copies for September 1988. The postal course is advertised nationally from 1988 and has twelve thousand pupils by 1990 at six pounds a month. Hadley's fund takes outside money from 1987 on the dull method and does well in the recovery. Two London houses in 1988, sold in 1989 before the fall. A sixth of the Muggle profit to the well-digging charity from 1986, chosen on two sentences.

Very rich by any ordinary standard at fourteen; small beside what the house is to its own world, which is the difficulty Jacob named. Nobody in the Muggle world knows the owner's age until 1993, when a Sunday paper finds out and Hadley says yes.

## What goes wrong

Written plainly, as things that happen to a workshop and a shop: materials, people, the law. Never comic, never a lesson, never Harry's carelessness. The consequence is real (a recall costs money; a scald costs a man's trust; a law costs a year) and is handled by the people whose job it is.
- 1986: a preserving charm on a batch of Pepperup preserves the wrong thing and the batch is recalled; Harry writes the recall notice himself and the weekly, later, prints every recall the house ever makes.
- 1987: the ward kit's card reports a crossing every time a cat crosses the line; a householder in Tinworth sits up all night with a wand; the kit is reworked to ignore anything under a stone in weight, which takes Ruth Marchbanks a month.
- 1987: Mr Adeyemi's first rig boils over and scalds Joan Tilley's arm; the house's first injury; Harry at St Mungo's; the rig rebuilt with a Muggle cut-out; the engineer nearly leaves and does not.
- 1988: the Ministry's draft law on the cards; a competitor's copy that does not work and burns a kitchen in Kent; the house's cards get a mark; the copy's maker sues and loses.
- 1988: the apothecaries' third complaint is to the Wizengamot and is about the heat ring, which they are all using.
- 1989: a child in Hogsmeade opens a stopper that should not have opened; nobody is hurt; the stoppers are re-charmed; Harry writes to the parents.
- 1990: the water-clearing pamphlet reaches a village in Wales where a Muggle-born witch has been clearing the pump for a year, and the Ministry charges her under the Statute, and the house pays for her defence, and the case is where the Statute question comes to a head.

## Named people who do the work (for the chapters)

- **Dennis**, the shop; later runs Hogsmeade.
- **Ruth Marchbanks** (no relation she will admit to), the warding reader, who draws the Gringotts door and later the ward kit.

- **The Hogsmeade charms master, Mr Blishwick**, retired, who makes the cards.
- **Hester Pye**, over all of it.

## What the chapters must show (not report)
A thing working for the first time in a room with people in it. A thing failing and someone hurt or frightened. Someone buying a thing and what it does for them. A fight with whoever the thing displaces. A figure said aloud by someone who has to say it. A recall notice. Harry reciting a page from 1611 to a man who then casts it.


## Staffing and directions (binding)

Research headcount (house total in brackets): Nov 1984, 6 (8); Mar 1985, 10 (14); Dec 1985, 16 (30); Dec 1986, 28 (50); Dec 1987, 50 (100), Oxford's forty Muggle staff separate; 1989, 70 (150); 1991, 80 (170); 1994, 100 (200). The limit is supply: about forty Hogwarts leavers a year, a quarter Muggle-born; by 1987 he has hired most of the capable unemployed, and takes retired wizards, Squibs for wandless work, Muggle-born leavers from Ireland and France, and trains leavers by brief. Every researcher writes a report a week; he reads all of them.

Directions: 1984–85, his own limits and the first list (counts, the reading device, warding in print, potions by batch and cost, the werewolf count, demand). 1986, what nobody uses (the 240 lost charms cast one by one; products from the ones that work; warding in practice; linked objects begun; St Mungo's records; dragon pox; Muggle chemistry with Snape). 1987, making and binding (cards, heat ring, ledger, automation, press, index, casting theory, the first bound spell and its etymology, the Floo survey, pedagogy, a legal unit of two). 1988–89, production research (owner-only and recording cards, fire charm, rigs for every common potion, broom cushioning, owl logistics, Floo map, short wands with Ollivander, soil, the Patronus with the Unspeakables, vaccination counted). 1990–91, scale and the state (the index everywhere, the Wizengamot record, Statute law, the course in French and German, Dementors read about only). Hogwarts: venom, Dementors, the five-year ward records, Time-Turner mechanics, the Chamber.

Not researched by the house: soul and mind magic beyond reading; the Dark Arts; anything needing the Restricted Section (asked of Dumbledore by title); anything on a person before an inert target; anything on himself.

A direction is chosen by the list: what is wrong, what it needs, whether the house can supply it, and the cost in people, who are the scarce thing.
