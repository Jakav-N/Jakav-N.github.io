# The Long Second — Style Guide & Continuity Notes

> Author's working notes. Spoiler-light but not spoiler-free.

## Voice

- Close third person on Harry; narrator sits just behind his eyes, dry and precise,
  capable of warmth. Interlude chapters/sections may use other POVs (labeled or
  clearly signaled).
- Rationalist texture: concrete numbers, honest uncertainty, visible reasoning —
  but *lighter* than HPMOR: more warmth, less lecture. A reasoning passage should
  end in a feeling, a joke, or a decision, never in a seminar.
- British idiom throughout (biscuits, fortnight, Year Four, maths, post). Era
  discipline: 1981–1998. No internet until it exists; computing is BBC Micros,
  teletext, libraries, letters.
- Humor: dry, structural (the comedy of a vast mind handling small problems), and
  from outside POVs (what he looks like at 1×). Never mean-spirited toward
  children; the Dursleys are weather systems and middle-management farce, not
  torturers on-page.
- Keep it lighter (author's directive): the horror of the premise is acknowledged,
  then *answered* — Harry solves boredom, loneliness, fear, as engineering
  problems with emotional costs admitted honestly and briefly.

## Rendering 200×

- At least one concrete time-arithmetic grace note per chapter; never an infodump.
- Device: a single spoken sentence interrupted by paragraphs of interior work,
  then the sentence resumes — use sparingly, for effect.
- Subjective durations named in honest units ("he spent what would have been a
  fortnight on it, between one stair and the next").
- His body is slow: he plans every word, cannot talk fast, reads at eye-speed
  (~5× adult), waits in queues for subjective days. Patience as learned knot-craft.
- Emotions run at 200× too and were mastered over subjective centuries:
  equanimity is his resting state, but it is an achievement, not an absence.

## Prose conventions (for the Markdown sources)

- `#` chapter heading: `# Chapter N — Title`.
- Scene breaks: `---` on its own line.
- Interlude sections begin with a line like `*Interlude: Number Four's doorstep*`
  (italic label) when the POV leaves Harry.
- Em dashes with no surrounding spaces (—like this). Proper curly quotes typed
  directly in the source. Thoughts in italics.
- Epigraphs (optional): blockquote at chapter top, attributed to the ciphered
  journals ("Journals, vol. —, translated") or in-world documents.
- Target 4,000–6,000 words per chapter.

## Continuity ledger

Maintained at the bottom of `outline.md`. Update after every chapter: facts
established, names coined, dates, promises to the reader.

## Canon discipline

- Dates anchor to canon: doorstep 1 Nov 1981; letter July 1991; Hogwarts
  1 Sept 1991.
- Canon events occur unless Harry's existence plausibly deflects them; deflections
  must be *caused*, never convenient. No luck. Any canon character acting
  differently does so because this Harry changed their incentives or information.
- Mechanism secrecy: the world may learn he is a genius; nobody may learn about
  the 200× without it being earned on the page.
